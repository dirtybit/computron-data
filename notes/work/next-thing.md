- **I04 — One-Tap “Why Did You Do That?”**  
Concept: Every proactive suggestion and automation has a single-tap “explain” panel: what triggered it, what data was used, and how to change it.  
Primary problem: Users feel loss of control; opaque automation reduces trust.  
Why it fits: Devices can set the standard for AI transparency UX (without org-wide changes).  
Dependency: Medium. Category: Trust / cross-device system layer. Startup signal: S6.



**I14 — Household “How-To Library”**  
Concept: A shared, household-owned knowledge base of “how we do things here” (Wi-Fi reset, pet feeding routine, trash day)—captured as short voice/video snippets and surfaced contextually when asked.  
Primary problem: Household knowledge is undocumented; new caregivers/guests struggle.  
Why it fits: A device-native memory feature with strong utility and low creep.  
Dependency: Low–Medium. Category: Home UX. Startup signal: S1 (teach mode), S3.

**I22 — Limited Gesture Micro-Controls**  
Concept: Small gesture vocabulary (wave to dismiss, hold palm to pause ambient) with strict constraints, visible affordances, and high confidence thresholds.  
Primary problem: Hands-busy moments need non-voice input.  
Why it fits: A device interaction layer; can be prototyped on specific SKUs first.  
Dependency: Medium. Category: Device / AI interface. Startup signal: none required.

**I23 — E‑Ink Peripheral Strip (Always-On, Low-Info)**  
Concept: A low-power strip display accessory (or Echo Hub variant) that shows only 3–5 household signals (next event, timer, weather, “quiet mode”), built for the periphery.  
Primary problem: Homes need “always there” info without a bright screen.  
Why it fits: Physical design + glanceable UI—core Devices & Design craft.  
Dependency: Medium. Category: Device / home UX. Startup signal: S11, S13.
