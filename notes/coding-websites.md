# Coding Exercise Websites for Seasoned Developers

Great alternatives to LeetCode when you're not interview-prepping — just want to stay sharp and have fun writing code.

---

### Codewars — Best "fun factor" pick
**[codewars.com](https://www.codewars.com)**

Community-authored exercises called "kata" with a martial-arts-inspired ranking system (8 kyu to 1 kyu). 55+ languages supported. The best part: after you
solve a problem, you can see how others solved it — great for picking up idioms and alternative approaches. Casual, fun, community-driven, and very low
friction — just pick a kata and go. You can also create your own kata based on your interests.

---

### CodinGame — Most unique / visual
**[codingame.com](https://www.codingame.com)**

You literally code through games with visual feedback. "Clash of Code" gives you 15-minute speed races against others. Bot programming challenges where
your code controls a game character. 25+ languages, 100% free, 3M+ users. Great for rekindling the joy of coding rather than grinding DSA patterns.

---

### Exercism — Best for craft / quality of code
**[exercism.org](https://www.exercism.org)**

8,000+ exercises across 82 languages. What sets it apart: human mentorship and focus on writing *idiomatic* code, not just correct code. As a seasoned
dev, the angle of writing elegant, idiomatic solutions rather than just passing test cases is refreshing — especially for brushing up on a language you
haven't used in a while. Free, CLI or browser-based.

---

### Advent of Code — Best seasonal tradition
**[adventofcode.com](https://adventofcode.com)**

25 puzzles every December, increasing in difficulty, tied together with a narrative storyline. Language-agnostic. Huge community posts solutions and
discussions afterward. Past years' problems are always available, so you can work through them anytime.

---

### Project Euler — Best for math + code intersection
**[projecteuler.net](https://projecteuler.net)**

Math-meets-programming problems. If you enjoy the intersection of algorithms and number theory, this is unmatched. Problems range from approachable to
research-paper-level difficulty. Language-agnostic, "inductive chain learning" where solving problems unlocks new concepts.

---

### Codeforces — For when you want a real challenge
**[codeforces.com](https://codeforces.com)**

Strong for competitive programming practice with regular contests that sharpen algorithmic thinking. Huge archive of past contest problems at all
difficulty levels, so you can pick your pain level. More serious than Codewars, but very respected in the competitive programming world.

---

### HackerRank — Solid middle ground
**[hackerrank.com](https://www.hackerrank.com)**

Vast library of challenges across multiple domains — algorithms, databases, AI, regex, shell scripting. Built-in IDE with multi-language support. Less
flashy but very reliable, and has more variety than LeetCode.

---

## Quick Recommendation

Since you're not chasing jobs, just want to stay sharp and have fun:

- **Codewars** for daily quick hits
- **CodinGame** when you want something visually stimulating
- **Exercism** when you want to explore a language deeply or write elegant code
- **Advent of Code** for a seasonal deep-dive
- **Project Euler** if you enjoy mathematical thinking

All free. LeetCode is still great for pure DSA but it leans heavily interview-focused, which is exactly what you don't need right now.
