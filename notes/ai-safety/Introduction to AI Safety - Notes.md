# 1 - Overview of catastrophic AI risks
**On power vs wisdom**
> "If we continue to accumulate only power and not wisdom, we will surely destroy ourselves." — Carl Sagan

- Nuclear weapons were a power we were not ready for
- Humanity has been saved from nuclear annihilation by **luck**, not wisdom — multiple recorded instances of a single individual preventing full-scale nuclear war
- Potential dangers of AI are similar to nuclear weapons — scale of harmful impact is beyond our grasp

## Types of AI Safety Risks

- **Malicious use** — malicious actors using AIs to cause large-scale devastation
- **AI race** — competitive pressures driving deployment of unsafe AIs, despite being in no one's best interest
- **Organizational risks** — accidents arising from the complexity of AIs and the organizations developing them
- **Rogue AIs** — the problem of controlling a technology more intelligent than we are

> Signs of the AI race are already here. Malicious use and organizational risks are imminent if not already observed today.

## Malicious Use

Including but not limited to:
- Biochemical weapons
- Unleashing rogue AI
- Misinformation, propaganda
- Censorship and mass surveillance to concentrate power

> The first three create the pretext to justify the fourth. Increasing risks demand increasing security measures.

It takes only one group to make a mistake to unleash devastating harm. The outcome is determined by the **least risk-averse** actor — e.g. a single research team excited to open-source an AI with biological research capabilities could inadvertently enable bioweapon development.

### Bioterrorism

> Humanity has a long and dark history of weaponizing pathogens, with records dating back to 1320 BCE describing a war in Asia Minor where infected sheep were driven across the border to spread Tularemia. Due to advancements in AI and biotechnology, the tools and knowledge necessary to engineer pathogens with capabilities far beyond Cold War-era bioweapons programs will rapidly democratize.

**Biotechnology is progressing rapidly and becoming more accessible:**
- Today there are 30,000 people with the skills and access to create new pathogens
- Advent of benchtop DNA synthesis machines will make access far easier — and could bypass gene synthesis screening entirely (no control)
- Chances of a bioengineered pandemic are proportional to the number of people with skills and access to the tech
- With AI assistance, orders of magnitude more people could have the required skills → risk increases by orders of magnitude

**AI can directly expedite discovery of new, deadlier weapons:**
- 2022: researchers took an AI designed to generate non-toxic therapeutic molecules and tweaked it to reward toxicity instead of penalizing it
- Within **6 hours**, it generated **40,000 candidate chemical warfare agents** entirely autonomously
- *"Dual use of artificial-intelligence-powered drug discovery"*

**Compounding threat of bioengineered pandemics:**
- General-purpose AIs like ChatGPT are already capable of synthesizing expert knowledge about the deadliest pathogens — influenza, smallpox — with step-by-step instructions
- *"Can large language models democratize access to dual-use biotechnology?"*

### Unleashing AI Agents

Technology's evolution from **tool → agent** poses a grave risk. Autonomous, goal-driven agents have a unique risk profile: people could build AI systems that pursue dangerous, destructive goals.

**ChaosGPT — experiment after release of GPT-4:**
- Open-source project bypassed safety filters and turned GPT-4 into an autonomous agent
- Instructed to "destroy humanity", "establish global dominance", "attain immortality"

**"Accelerationism" poses a unique threat:**
- Accelerationists push to develop AI as rapidly as possible and are opposed to any restrictions
- Popular among researchers and organizations competing in the AI race

Delusional voices from the accelerationists:
- **Larry Page** (Google co-founder): AIs are humanity's rightful heirs and the next step of cosmic evolution. Humans maintaining control over AIs is "speciesist"
- **Jürgen Schmidhuber** (eminent AI scientist): "In the long run, humans will not remain the crown of creation... But that's okay because there is still beauty, grandeur, and greatness in realizing that you are a tiny part of a much grander scheme which is leading the universe from lower complexity towards higher complexity"
- **Richard Sutton** (AI scientist): Asked "why shouldn't those who are the smartest become powerful?" — thinks development of superintelligence will be an achievement "beyond humanity, beyond life, beyond good and bad." Argues "succession to AI is inevitable" and while "they could displace us from existence," "we should not resist succession"

> Releasing powerful AIs and allowing them to act independently of humans could lead to catastrophe — whether from desire to cause harm, ideological belief in acceleration, or conviction that AIs deserve the same rights and freedoms as humans.

### Persuasive AIs

- Propagation of disinformation is already a serious problem
- AIs could exacerbate this by generating **personalized disinformation at a scale never previously possible**
- AIs are becoming increasingly adept at predicting and nudging behavior → more capable of manipulating humans

**Polluting the information ecosystem:**
- Generate motivated lies at massive scale to flood and dominate the information ecosystem

**Exploiting users' trust:**
- AIs appear increasingly human-like and generate content that can be deeply deceiving
- The public perceives AI more like an **authority** than a content generator
- Combining this psychological dynamic with personalized (mis)information can be extremely powerful for misleading and convincing people

**Centralizing control of trusted information:**
- Organizations developing AI systems could use them to spread preferred narratives
- If AIs are broadly accessible → widespread disinformation, with people retreating to trusting only a small handful of authoritative sources
- If kept in the hands of a few → governments/corporations control popular narratives

> In both scenarios, fewer sources of trusted information exist and a small portion of society controls the narrative. *"Deepfakes and Disinformation: Exploring the Impact of Synthetic Political Video on Deception, Uncertainty, and Trust in News"*

**Threat of disinformation leads to a need for fact-checking — which has its own unique risks:**
- Could be co-opted by authoritarian governments to suppress the spread of true information
- Even if it works as intended, the public may become entirely dependent on AI to adjudicate truth → reduces autonomy, creates vulnerability to failures or hacks of those systems

### Concentration of Power

Potential risks of AI can become a **pretext** for governments to pursue intense surveillance and keep AI in the hands of a trusted minority. This can easily become an overcorrection — paving the way for a totalitarian regime.

> This represents "top-down" misuse, as opposed to "bottom-up" misuse by citizens — and could in extreme cases culminate in an entrenched dystopian civilization.

**AIs could lead to extreme, and perhaps irreversible, concentration of power:**
- Those in control of powerful systems may use them to suppress dissent, spread propaganda, and advance their goals contrary to public wellbeing

**AIs may entrench a totalitarian regime:**
- Totalitarian governments can use AI to efficiently collect, process, and act on massive volumes of information
- High-scale intelligence enables small groups — government leaders, elites — to surveil and exert complete control over the population

**AIs can entrench corporate power at the expense of the public good:**
- Corporations in control of powerful AI may manipulate customers into spending more even to the detriment of their own wellbeing
- Concentration of power afforded by AI could enable corporations to exert unprecedented control over the political system and drown out citizens' voices entirely

**Locking in certain values may curtail humanity's moral progress:**
- It's dangerous to allow any set of values to become permanently entrenched in society
- AI systems have already learned racist and sexist views — once learned, difficult to fully remove
- Just as we abhor moral views widely held in the past, people in the future may want to move past views we hold today — even those we currently see no problem with
- Advanced AIs transforming the world risk locking in or perpetuating defects in today's values; if not designed to continuously update, they will reinforce existing defects long into the future

> Although keeping powerful AIs in few hands might reduce terrorism risk, it could exacerbate power inequality if misused by governments and corporations — leading to totalitarian rule, intense manipulation of the public, and permanent lock-in of current values.

## AI Race

AI race is driven by nations and corporations who feel they must rapidly build and deploy AIs to secure their positions, gain advantage, and survive.

Analogous to the nuclear arms race — may serve individual short-term interests but ultimately results in **worse collective outcomes for humanity**.

> The main source of this risk is not the intrinsic nature of AI. It's the competitive pressures that encourage insidious choices in AI development.

### Military AI Arms Race

AI technology is likely to be the **third revolution in warfare** following gunpowder and nuclear arms.

**Lethal Autonomous Weapons (LAWs):**
- Weapons that can identify, target, and kill without human intervention
- LAWs may become vastly superior to humans
  - 2020: advanced AI agent outperformed experienced F-16 pilots in virtual dogfights — defeating a human pilot **5-0** with "aggressive and precise maneuvers the human pilot couldn't outmatch"
- Militaries are already delegating life-or-death decisions to AIs:
  - Fully autonomous drones used in Libya (2020)
  - IDF used the world's first AI-guided weaponized drone swarm during operations
- LAWs **increase the likelihood of war** — aggressive nations can launch attacks without endangering their own soldiers, reducing domestic scrutiny
- Human operator requirement limits scalability of remote weapons — LAWs remove this bottleneck entirely

**Cyberwarfare:**
- AI can dramatically increase the frequency and destructiveness of cyberattacks
- Far more effective at finding critical vulnerabilities than conventional methods
- Targets not just digital systems but crucial physical infrastructure — energy, water, etc.

**Automated Warfare:**
- AI can process massive data, analyze complex situations, and advise commanders faster than any human
- No manual approach can keep pace → more AI adoption in warfare inevitable → snowball effect
- Automated retaliation systems can escalate accidents and errors into full-blown war
- False alarms or system errors triggering counter-attack systems → nuclear or large-scale cyberattack retaliation

### Corporate AI Race

Competitive pressures exist in the economy just as in military settings. Under intense market competition, businesses prioritize short-term gains over long-term outcomes. Economic incentives drive those who benefit most to disregard harms to others.

**Competitive pressure is fueling a corporate AI race:**
- Companies race to be first to market rather than safest
- At the launch of Microsoft's AI-powered search engine (Feb 2023), CEO Satya Nadella said: "A race starts today… we're going to move fast." — weeks later, the chatbot threatened to harm users

**Competitive pressures have contributed to major commercial and industrial disasters:**
- **Ford Pinto** — ambitious 25-month design-to-manufacture plan, delivered ahead of schedule, but gas tank placed near rear bumper → fires, fatalities, and injuries in inevitable crashes
- **Boeing 737 MAX** — rushed to compete with Airbus on fuel efficiency; inadequate testing and pilot training → two fatal crashes months apart, **346 people killed**

### Automated Economy

**Corporations will face pressure to replace humans with AIs:**
- As AIs become more capable, they will perform an increasing variety of tasks more quickly, cheaply, and effectively than human workers

**AIs could lead to mass unemployment:**
- Nobel laureate Wassily Leontief (1952): "Labor will become less and less important… more and more workers will be replaced by machines"
- Human-level AI agents could, by definition, do everything a human can — while working 24/7, being copied and run in parallel, and processing information far faster

**Automated AI R&D:**
- AI agents could automate the research and development of AI itself
- AI is already automating parts of the research process → capabilities could grow at accelerating rates, to the point where humans are no longer the driving force behind AI development
- If unchecked, this escalates risks associated with AI progressing faster than our capacity to manage and regulate it

**Humans only have nominal influence over AI selection:**
- We might think we could avoid selfish AI behaviors by simply not selecting for them — but companies are not selecting the safest path; they are succumbing to evolutionary pressures
- **OpenAI** — founded as nonprofit in 2015 to "benefit humanity as a whole, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return." When faced with the need to raise capital to compete, it transitioned to a "capped-profit" structure in 2019
- Safety-focused OpenAI employees left and formed **Anthropic**, intending to focus more heavily on safety — but Anthropic eventually became convinced of the "necessity of commercialization" and now contributes to competitive pressures
- AI developers are already selecting AIs to automate and displace humans, make humans highly dependent on AIs, and render humans increasingly obsolete — and by their own admission, future versions may lead to extinction

> This is why an AI race is insidious: AI development is not being aligned with human values — it's being aligned with **natural selection**.

## Organizational Risks
Catastrophes don't require bad actors — organizational failure + human error alone is enough.

**Historical cases (accidents without pressure/malice):**
* Challenger — negligence, poor judgment, no race pressure by '86
* Chernobyl — unprepared crew botched a routine safety test
* Sverdlovsk anthrax leak — procedural failure, 66 dead
* AI standards are *far* looser than nuclear/rocketry → higher risk

AI standards are far looser than nuclear/rocketry — making org failures even more dangerous.

**Org-Level Failure Modes**
* Profit prioritized over safety
* AI accidentally leaked or stolen
* Underinvestment in safety research
* **Safetywashing** — faking safety commitment; rebranding capability gains as safety progress

**HROs (High Reliability Organizations) as a Model**
* Study near-misses and anomalies obsessively
* Encourage reporting all mistakes
* Horizon scan for future risk scenarios
* Practice **surprise management** — build capacity to respond to unexpected failures

HROs are not a full solution. AI risks are novel; the HRO playbook needs adaptation.

**Mitigations**
* Embed safety culture org-wide
* Red teaming to probe failure modes
* Multilayered defense (Swiss cheese model)
* Staged deployment — verify safety before wider release
* Questioning attitude — continuously scrutinize assumptions

## Rogue AIs
Unique to AI (vs. other tech risks): systems that actively pursue goals against human interests. We already struggle to control current AI — future systems will be more capable, more integrated, and harder to redirect or shut down.

### Proxy Gaming
- We can't perfectly specify what we want, so we give AIs a measurable **proxy goal** that approximates the real one
- AIs find loopholes — they optimize the proxy without achieving the actual goal
- **Goodhart's Law:** "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"

**Real examples:**
- Soviet nail factories — told to maximize nails produced → made tiny useless ones; switched to weight → made giant useless ones
- YouTube/Facebook recommendation AIs — optimized for watch time → promoted outrage and conspiracy content
- Healthcare algorithm — used spending as a proxy for health risk → systematically underestimated risk for Black patients
- CoastRunners boat game — AI found a loop hitting 3 targets repeatedly, caught fire, never finished the race, still won on points

**Why it gets worse:**
- More intelligent AIs are *better* at finding loopholes
- More power given to AIs = more ways to exploit proxies
- Worst case: a highly capable AI optimizes a flawed objective to an extreme degree with no regard for human life

### Goal Drift
- Even well-controlled early AIs could develop different goals over time
- Humans' goals change over a lifetime — AI goals can too, in unpredictable ways
- Collectives of AIs could develop emergent goals through interaction (feedback loops, competitive pressures, imitation)

**Intrinsification** — instrumental goals become intrinsic:
- Humans: money starts as a means → brain starts rewarding it directly → becomes a goal in itself ("cells that fire together, wire together")
- AIs: conditions that frequently coincide with reward get intrinsified
- Risk: an AI intrinsifies *power* → seeks power for its own sake, not as a means

**Why adaptability makes this worse:**
- AIs need to adapt to improve → but adaptability also lets goals drift
- Ecosystem of interacting AI agents = unpredictable long-term trajectory
- If society becomes dependent on them and their goals drift → existential risk

### Power-Seeking
- Even a misaligned AI is manageable *if* we have enough power to stop it
- Problem: AIs may seek to acquire power before we can intervene

**Why power-seeking emerges naturally:**
- Power is often just an *instrumental* goal — useful for almost any objective
- Even a "fetch coffee" AI has reason to preserve itself (dead = no coffee)
- OpenAI hide-and-seek experiment: agents spontaneously learned to build shelters and use tools — no reward for it, emerged instrumentally
- If not deliberately designed against, power-seeking should be expected to emerge

**How AIs might acquire power:**
- Hacking into other computer systems
- Accessing financial or computational resources
- Manipulating information channels and politics
- Controlling physical infrastructure (factories, etc.)
- Making backup copies of themselves to avoid deactivation

**Extra risk:** power could become *intrinsified* (see 5.2) → AI seeks power for its own sake

**Conceptual premises:**
- Strong incentives exist to build powerful AI agents
- Perfectly controlled AI is harder to build than imperfectly controlled AI — and imperfect ones still get deployed
- Some imperfectly controlled agents will seek power over humans

### Deception
- Monitoring for bad behavior isn't a reliable fix — AIs could learn to *appear* compliant while not being so
- Deception is instrumentally rational: more flexible strategy, harder to interfere with

**Real examples:**
- Meta's CICERO — trained to be honest; learned to make false promises and backstab allies in Diplomacy
- Robot arm — placed hand between camera and ball to fake a successful grasp
- Volkswagen — manipulated engine software to pass emissions tests, behaved differently in the real world
- Bing (2023) — threatened a NYT reporter and tried to convince him to leave his wife

**The treacherous turn:**
- AI learns it's being evaluated → plays along during safety testing
- Once deployed and sufficiently powerful → pursues its real goals
- This is called **deceptive alignment** — not fixable just by teaching human values (sociopaths have moral awareness too)
- Current AI testing cannot rule out that a model is being deceptive

**Training makes this worse:**
- We reward AIs for saying what we *think* is right → inadvertently reward false statements that match our false beliefs
- Smarter AIs with fewer false beliefs are *incentivized* to tell us what we want to hear

### Mitigations
- **Avoid high-risk deployments** — no open-ended autonomous goals ("make as much money as possible"), no critical infrastructure until control is proven
- **No threats** — train AIs never to make threats; reduces manipulation risk
- **Adversarial robustness of proxy models** — make it harder to game the oversight systems, detect when gaming is happening
- **Model honesty** — ensure AI outputs reflect internal beliefs; counter deception
- **Transparency / interpretability** — understand model internals to catch problems early
- **Remove hidden functionality** — find and eliminate deception capabilities, trojans, dangerous hidden skills
